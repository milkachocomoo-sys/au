✅ Autism-friendly rules followed on this site

  • 🎨
    Muted palette: soft sage greens and dusty blues — no bright or saturated colors
  • 💡
    Off-white background (#f5f2eb): reduces glare compared to pure white
  • 🚫
    No animations or autoplay: nothing moves unless you click it
  • ♿
    prefers-reduced-motion respected: all transitions disabled if the OS setting is on
  • 🔤
    Atkinson Hyperlegible font: designed specifically for low-vision and dyslexic readers
  • 📏
    18px+ body text, 1.7 line-height: comfortable to read without strain
  • 📐
    Single-column layout: one clear path, no distractions
  • 🏷️
    Icons always paired with text: never icon alone
  • 👆
    Large click targets (44–48px min): easy to tap on mobile
  • ⌨️
    Keyboard accessible: skip link, visible focus rings, logical tab order
  • 💬
    Plain language: no idioms, no sarcasm, no implied meaning
  • 🎛️
    User controls: dark mode + larger text — always visible at the top
  • ⏱️
    No time limits: forms have no countdown, no session pressure
  • 🗺️
    Predictable navigation: same nav on every page, breadcrumb trail, logo always goes home

About Calm Garden

Calm Garden is a website designed with autism-friendly and sensory-inclusive principles in mind. Every design decision has a reason behind it.

This site is a demonstration. It was created as a student project to show how websites can be made more comfortable and accessible for autistic and neurodivergent users.

What is universal design?

Universal design means creating products and systems that work for as many people as possible — without needing special adaptations. When we design for people who face the most barriers, the result is usually better for everyone.

For example: captions on videos help people who are deaf, but also people in noisy environments. Clear, plain language helps autistic users, but also anyone who is tired or reading in their second language.

Our design principles

  • 🎨
    Calm colours. Soft, muted tones only. No bright neons, no harsh colour combinations.
  • 🚫
    No surprise movement. Nothing animates, flashes, or auto-plays. Everything stays still until you choose to interact.
  • 📖
    Clear language. Written in plain English. No sarcasm, no idioms, no unclear references.
  • 🗺️
    Predictable structure. The navigation is the same on every page. You always know where you are and how to go back.
  • 🎛️
    User control. You can switch to dark mode or increase text size at any time using the buttons at the top.

Practical information and tips for everyday life. Each section covers a different topic. You can read them in any order.

👂 Managing sensory experiences

  • 🎧
    Noise-cancelling headphones can reduce overwhelming sounds in public spaces like shops, trains, or offices.
  • 🕶️
    Tinted glasses or a cap can reduce the effect of bright overhead lights, including fluorescent lighting.
  • 🧸
    Fidget tools give your hands something to do and can help with focus and self-regulation.
  • 🗺️
    Planning routes in advance can reduce the stress of new or unpredictable environments.
  • 🏠
    A calm space at home — a quiet room with dim lighting and familiar objects — can help recovery after overwhelming experiences.

📅 Routines and predictability

  • 📋
    Visual schedules show the day's activities in order and reduce uncertainty about what comes next.
  • ⏰
    Transition warnings — giving a 5-minute notice before changing activity — can make transitions easier.
  • 📱
    Task management apps such as Todoist or a simple notes app can help structure the day.

💬 Communication

  • ✍️
    Written communication (text or email) gives more time to process and respond, without real-time pressure.
  • 🃏
    Communication cards with pre-written phrases can help in situations where speaking is difficult.
  • 🗣️
    Scripts for common situations (ordering food, asking for help) can reduce anxiety in social interactions.
